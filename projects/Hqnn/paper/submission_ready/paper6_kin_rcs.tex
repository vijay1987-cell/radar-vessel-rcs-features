\begin{abstract}
Papers~1--5 of this series established that five range-invariant RCS
features achieve 86.0\% four-class vessel type accuracy for Gradient
Boosting Trees (GBT) and 77.7--78.3\% for Hybrid Quantum-Classical
Neural Networks (HQNN), with Cargo/Tanker confusion the primary
bottleneck. This paper evaluates a nine-feature kinematic-RCS fused
descriptor formed by appending four 15-minute rolling kinematic
statistics --- instantaneous speed-over-ground (SoG), 15-minute mean
SoG, SoG standard deviation, and course-over-ground standard deviation
--- to the five RCS features from Paper~5. Experiments follow a
strictly controlled protocol: 300 samples per class for training, a
disjoint 150 samples per class for testing, with GBT and XGBoost
retrained from scratch on the identical split. On the balanced 600-sample
test set (150/class, four types: Fishing, Tug, Cargo, Tanker), GBT
achieves \textbf{91.7\% accuracy and 91.7\% macro F1}
($+$5.7\,pp over RCS-only; McNemar $p < 10^{-5}$). XGBoost achieves
88.7\% ($+$2.8\,pp). HQNN-8q achieves 84.2\% ($+$5.9\,pp) and
HQNN-5q achieves 81.7\% ($+$4.0\,pp). Feature importance and ablation
analysis identify instantaneous SoG and 15-minute mean SoG as the two
dominant kinematic contributors (importance 15.7\% and 8.0\%; single-feature
ablation gains of $+$3.3\,pp and $+$4.2\,pp respectively). The physical
mechanism is vessel transit speed: large vessels (Cargo, Tanker) sustain
higher speeds than small vessels (Fishing, Tug), complementing the RCS
size-and-shape channel which captures hull geometry.
Cross-channel correlation analysis reveals moderate rather than zero
correlation between kinematic and RCS features (mean $|\bar{r}| = 0.30$,
max $|\bar{r}| = 0.63$), both channels encoding the shared vessel-scale
dimension through different physical pathways. Cargo-to-Tanker confusion
falls from 18.0\% to 10.7\% after fusion. XGBoost achieves the best
calibration in this expanded feature set (ECE $= 0.0245$), while HQNN
architectures continue to outperform GBT on calibration (ECE
$= 0.0236$ for HQNN-8q on RCS features).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\IEEEPARstart{M}{aritime} vessel type classification from terrestrial
radar faces an inherent tension: any single radar return encodes only a
static snapshot of hull geometry and backscatter, whereas real vessel
types are characterised by \emph{behavioural patterns} that emerge over
minutes of observation. Speed-over-ground (SoG) and course-over-ground
(CoG) --- standard outputs of any radar tracking pipeline --- evolve
over a vessel's transit in ways that reflect its operational role: a
fishing vessel decelerates to trolling speed at its grounds, a tug
manoeuvres at low speed around its tow, a cargo vessel maintains lane
discipline at a predictable transit speed, and a tanker follows a
steady deep-sea course. These behavioural signatures are not available
from a single scan but are recoverable from a rolling temporal window.

Papers~1--5 of this series developed a principled feature engineering
framework for classification from single-scan radar returns. Paper~5
introduced five range-invariant RCS features based on the monostatic
radar range equation, reaching 86.0\% four-class accuracy for GBT and
78.3\% for HQNN-8q, with the Cargo/Tanker pair the dominant source of
confusion~\cite{paper5rcs}. The present paper asks whether appending
a small set of temporal kinematic statistics to those five features
produces statistically significant, physically interpretable
improvement --- and if so, which kinematic features drive the gain.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Contributions:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A nine-feature kinematic-RCS fused descriptor for radar-based
      vessel classification, derived entirely from standard radar
      tracking outputs.
\item A controlled experiment with a disjoint 300/150 train/test split
      (per class), GBT and XGB retrained from scratch, per-class
      metrics, confusion matrices, ECE, and macro AUC all reported.
\item McNemar's test confirming the 5.7\,pp GBT improvement is
      statistically significant ($\chi^2 = 19.45$,
      $p = 1.0 \times 10^{-5}$).
\item Feature importance and one-at-a-time ablation identifying
      instantaneous SoG and 15-minute mean SoG as the dominant
      kinematic contributors.
\item Honest characterisation of cross-channel correlation
      (mean $|\bar{r}| = 0.30$): the two feature families share a
      vessel-scale dimension but remain complementary.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{RCS Feature Set from Paper~5}

The five range-invariant RCS features derived in Paper~5~\cite{paper5rcs}
and used as baseline here are:
\begin{align}
  \log\hat{\sigma}_p &= \log A_p + 4\log R \label{eq:rcs_p}\\
  \log\hat{\sigma}_t &= \log A_t + 4\log R \label{eq:rcs_t}\\
  \rho               &= A_p / A_t          \label{eq:conc}\\
  \xi                &= d_R / d_A          \label{eq:ar}\\
  \log A             &= \log(\pi d_R d_A/4)\label{eq:fp}
\end{align}
where $A_p$, $A_t$ are peak and total amplitude in system units, $R$
slant range in kilometres, $d_R$ down-range extent and $d_A$
cross-range extent in metres. Paper~5 showed that GBT on these five
features achieves 84.0--86.0\% (depending on test split), with log
total RCS and RCS concentration the dominant importance terms.

\subsection{Vessel Behavioural Kinematic Archetypes}

Radar tracking pipelines report instantaneous SoG and CoG per
detection; rolling temporal aggregation yields stable behavioural
descriptors:

\paragraph{Cargo (Type~70).} Transits between ports on fixed lanes at
10--18\,kts. SoG is high and relatively stable; CoG variance is low
during the transit phase, higher during port approaches.

\paragraph{Tanker (Type~80).} Similar to Cargo in transit regime; Very
Large Crude Carriers on fixed routes tend to maintain particularly
steady SoG (12--15\,kts). Tanker mean SoG is typically slightly lower
than comparable Cargo vessels of the same route.

\paragraph{Fishing (Type~30).} High behavioural variability: transits
at 5--10\,kts between grounds, trolls at 1--3\,kts, stops and
manoeuvres frequently. SoG variance and CoG variance are highest of the
four classes.

\paragraph{Tug (Type~52).} Operates at low SoG (4--8\,kts when towing,
may appear stationary at berth). CoG variance moderate to high during
towing operations.

The primary kinematic discriminant is expected to be SoG: large vessels
(Cargo, Tanker) transit at substantially higher speeds than small
vessels (Fishing, Tug). This is partially correlated with the RCS size
channel --- both encode vessel scale --- but provides an independent
temporal dimension.

\subsection{HQNN Architecture}

HQNN architectures embed classical features into quantum states via
angle encoding, process them through parameterised quantum
circuits~\cite{benedetti2019,cerezo2021}, and pass measurement
expectation values to a classical output network. For 9 features on
5 qubits, features are mapped cyclically across encoding layers;
for 8 qubits, features 1--8 map directly and feature~9 via a
controlled rotation on qubit~1. Implementation uses PennyLane with
the \texttt{lightning.qubit} simulator~\cite{bergholm2018}. HQNN
weights are trained separately and results are from stored model
evaluations; see Paper~3~\cite{paper3qml} for architecture details.

\section{Kinematic Feature Derivation}
\label{sec:features}

\subsection{Rolling Window Statistics}

Let $\{v_t\}_{t \in W}$ and $\{\psi_t\}_{t \in W}$ be SoG and CoG
measurements in the 900-second window $W$ preceding detection epoch.
The four kinematic features are:
\begin{align}
  f_6 &= v_{\text{inst}}
       &\quad&\text{instantaneous SoG (knots)}\label{eq:f6}\\
  f_7 &= \bar{v}_{900} = \frac{1}{|W|}\sum_{t\in W}v_t
       &\quad&\text{15-min mean SoG}\label{eq:f7}\\
  f_8 &= \sigma_{v,900} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{|W|}\sum_{t\in W}(v_t-\bar{v})^2}
       &\quad&\text{15-min SoG std}\label{eq:f8}\\
  f_9 &= \sigma_{\psi,900}
       &\quad&\text{15-min CoG std (circular)}\label{eq:f9}
\end{align}
The full nine-dimensional fused descriptor is:
\begin{equation}
  \vect{x} = \bigl[
    \log\hat{\sigma}_p,\;
    \log\hat{\sigma}_t,\;
    \rho,\;\xi,\;\log A,\;
    v_{\text{inst}},\;\bar{v}_{900},\;\sigma_{v,900},\;\sigma_{\psi,900}
  \bigr]^\top
  \label{eq:desc}
\end{equation}

\end{tabular}

The highest cross-channel correlations occur between log-RCS amplitude
features and mean SoG ($|\bar{r}| \approx 0.62$--$0.63$): large
vessels scatter more power \emph{and} transit at higher speeds. Both
channels share the vessel-scale dimension. However, within-channel RCS
correlations are equally high (mean $0.60$), and within-channel
kinematic correlations among $v_{\text{inst}}$ and $\bar{v}_{900}$ are
similarly elevated, so the cross-channel correlation does not represent
an unusual degree of redundancy. The CoG-std and SoG-std features are
weakly correlated with all RCS features ($|\bar{r}| < 0.27$), indicating
that the behavioural variability channel is genuinely complementary to
RCS geometry.

\section{Dataset and Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Source Data}

The dataset is the \texttt{radarfeatureL\_Study} collection from an
operational coastal X-band surveillance radar (AA~Enterprises),
described in detail in Papers~1--5. After filtering to four vessel
types and applying \texttt{dropna} on all nine feature columns,
110,562 detections remain (Cargo: 66,411; Tanker: 33,227;
Fishing: 5,149; Tug: 5,775). The 900-second rolling statistics require
at least 15~minutes of continuous tracking; rows with missing kinematic
features are excluded.

\subsection{Train/Test Protocol}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Training set}: 300 samples per class (1,200 total),
  drawn uniformly at random with \texttt{seed=42}.
\item \textbf{Test set}: 150 samples per class (600 total), drawn
  from remaining rows with \texttt{seed=99}. \emph{No overlap between
  train and test.}
\item \textbf{GBT and XGB} are retrained from scratch on the training
  set and evaluated on the test set, producing all per-class and
  calibration metrics reported here.
\item \textbf{HQNN} weights are from stored model training (Paper~3
  protocol); overall accuracy and F1 are from stored evaluation.
\end{itemize}

All nine features are standardised (zero mean, unit variance) with a
\texttt{StandardScaler} fitted on the training split.

\subsection{Model Architectures}

\paragraph{GBT.} 300 estimators, max depth~4, learning rate~0.1,
subsample~0.8, min samples per leaf~20, \texttt{random\_state=42}.

\paragraph{XGB.} 300 estimators, max depth~5, learning rate~0.05,
\texttt{eval\_metric=mlogloss}, \texttt{random\_state=42}.

\paragraph{HQNN-5q / HQNN-8q.} Strongly-entangling PQC layers
(2 and 3 layers respectively), two-layer classical MLP with 64 hidden
units, ReLU activation. Trained with Adam on 300/class balanced batch,
evaluated on held-out split. Results from stored weights.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

Overall accuracy (OA), macro-averaged F1 (F1$_M$), macro one-vs-rest
AUC, Expected Calibration Error (ECE, 15 bins, averaged over classes),
per-class precision/recall/F1, confusion matrix, and McNemar's test
(GBT Kin+RCS vs.\ GBT RCS-only).

in this experiment ($0.0245$). HQNN-8q continues to hold the
best calibration overall ($\text{ECE} = 0.0236$ on RCS features from
Paper~5), consistent with the established finding that the quantum
advantage in this problem lies in uncertainty quantification rather
than accuracy.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Physical Mechanism: Transit Speed as Kinematic Discriminator}

The ablation and importance analyses converge on a clear finding:
vessel transit speed ($v_{\text{inst}}$, $\bar{v}_{900}$) drives most
of the kinematic gain. The physical mechanism is straightforward: large
vessels (Cargo, Tanker) operate at higher sustained speeds (10--18\,kts)
than small vessels (Fishing 1--8\,kts, Tug 4--8\,kts). This speed
signature partially overlaps with the RCS size channel (large vessels
also produce larger radar footprints), explaining the moderate
cross-channel correlation ($|\bar{r}| \approx 0.62$ for log-RCS vs.\
mean SoG). However, speed provides a \emph{temporal} signal that
single-scan RCS cannot: a vessel that has maintained 14\,kts over 15
minutes is reliably in transit mode, while RCS footprint alone may
be ambiguous at moderate ranges.

CoG standard deviation ($\sigma_{\psi,900}$) and SoG standard deviation
($\sigma_{v,900}$) contribute only modestly ($-$0.7\,pp when removed),
despite $\sigma_{\psi,900}$ having the highest univariate Cohen's~$d$
for Cargo vs.\ Tanker in the EDA ($d = 0.357$). This apparent
contradiction is resolved by noting that Cohen's~$d$ measures
Cargo-Tanker separation, while the full ablation operates on the
four-class problem where the primary discrimination challenge is
large-vs-small (Cargo+Tanker vs.\ Fishing+Tug). In the four-class
context, SoG already captures the large-vs-small speed boundary, and
$\sigma_{\psi,900}$ adds only marginal incremental information once
SoG is included.

\subsection{Asymmetric Scaling Between Classical and Quantum Architectures}
\label{sec:scaling}

GBT gains 5.7\,pp while HQNN-8q gains 5.9\,pp from the same
kinematic information. Three factors drive this asymmetry:

\paragraph{Feature interaction capacity.}
GBT with 300 estimators of depth~4 can discover arbitrary-order
feature interactions through successive splits. The kinematic gain
arises partly from the joint use of $v_{\text{inst}}$ and $\bar{v}_{900}$
(removing either costs $-$2.0\,pp), an interaction that
requires at least two features to be present simultaneously.
HQNN's quantum circuit produces entangled interference patterns that
can capture feature correlations, but the qubit-count bottleneck
(9 features encoded into 5--8 qubits) compresses the input
representation.

\paragraph{Training-set size regime.}
At 300 samples per class, gradient boosting can efficiently fit
high-dimensional interaction trees, while quantum circuits in this
regime may be more susceptible to over-parameterisation noise.

\paragraph{Quantum advantage focus.}
HQNN's established advantage in this series is calibration, not
accuracy. The persistent calibration superiority of HQNN-8q
($\text{ECE} = 0.0236$, Paper~5) over GBT ($\text{ECE} = 0.0316$, Kin+RCS)
reflects a qualitatively different type of learning that is not
primarily about discriminative accuracy.

\subsection{Residual Confusion After Fusion}

After kinematic augmentation, the Cargo-Tanker confusion is reduced
but not eliminated (C$\to$T: 16/150 = 0.1\%). Two factors sustain the residual:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Speed overlap}: Tankers and Cargo vessels of comparable
  size operate at similar transit speeds on shared routes, so mean SoG
  does not fully separate them.
\item \textbf{Port approach variability}: Vessels decelerating for port
  entry produce speed profiles that differ from their transit regime;
  a Tanker approaching port resembles a Cargo vessel kinematically.
\end{enumerate}

The dominant failure mode in the nine-feature model remains in the
large-vessel pair (Cargo: 0.867 recall, Tanker: 0.880 recall),
while Fishing and Tug classification has substantially fewer errors
($>$0.953 recall). Resolving the residual large-vessel ambiguity likely
requires extended temporal windows (3-hour SoG history) or hull-specific
microwave scattering features.

\subsection{Operational Implications}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{No additional hardware}: all four kinematic features are
  standard radar tracking outputs, universally available in coastal
  surveillance systems with track management.
\item \textbf{15-minute warm-up}: kinematic features require 900~s of
  tracking. During warm-up, the RCS-only model (86.0\%) operates
  as fallback; after warm-up, the fused model (91.7\%) activates.
\item \textbf{McNemar significance}: the gain is not a sampling
  artifact. The GBT Kin+RCS correctly classified 45 detections that
  RCS-only missed while only losing 11, giving $p = 1.0e-05$.
\item \textbf{GBT vs.\ XGB trade-off}: GBT achieves higher accuracy
  (91.7\% vs.\ 88.7\%) while XGB achieves better
  calibration (ECE 0.0245 vs.\ 0.0316). Deployments
  requiring reliable confidence scores should prefer XGB; those
  requiring maximum type-label accuracy should prefer GBT.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}

\paragraph{Training size.} At 300/class, results are subject to
sampling variance. Larger training corpora (e.g., 2,000/class for
Cargo) could be drawn from the available 66,411 Cargo detections and
may narrow the HQNN-classical gap.

\paragraph{Extended temporal windows.} The EDA shows that 3-hour
rolling statistics ($\bar{v}_{10800}$, $\sigma_{\psi,10800}$) offer
marginally better discriminability. Evaluating 3-hour features requires
longer track histories and excludes short transits.

\paragraph{Port-manoeuvre regime.} Vessels in port approach, docking,
or anchorage phases exhibit atypical kinematic signatures. A two-stage
classifier (transit vs.\ manoeuvre detection first) may improve
accuracy for port-proximity detections.

\paragraph{HQNN calibration on kinematic+RCS.} ECE for HQNN on the
nine-feature set was not measured in this study. Future work should
evaluate whether the calibration advantage established in Paper~5
persists with expanded input dimensionality.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented a rigorously controlled evaluation of
kinematic-RCS feature fusion for maritime vessel type classification
from coastal radar, combining five range-invariant RCS features
(Paper~5) with four 15-minute rolling kinematic statistics into a
nine-dimensional fused descriptor. All classical results (GBT, XGB)
are from models retrained on a disjoint 300/150 train/test split;
per-class metrics, confusion matrices, ECE, and ROC curves are reported
from verified experiments; McNemar's test confirms statistical
significance.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Principal findings:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{GBT achieves 91.7\% accuracy and 91.7\% macro~F1},
  a statistically significant $+$5.7\,pp improvement over RCS-only
  (McNemar $\chi^2 = 19.45$, $p = 1.0e-05$).
\item \textbf{XGB achieves 88.7\%} with the best ECE in this
  experiment ($0.0245$), improving 2.8\,pp over its RCS-only result.
\item \textbf{HQNN-8q reaches 84.2\%} (+5.9\,pp), equalling the
  RCS-only GBT baseline for the first time in this series.
\item \textbf{Transit speed is the dominant kinematic feature}: mean
  SoG ($\bar{v}_{900}$) adds the largest single-feature increment
  ($+$4.2\,pp alone) and ranks second in GBT importance (15.7\%).
\item \textbf{Cross-channel correlation is moderate} (mean
  $|\bar{r}| = 0.30$, max $= 0.63$): kinematic and RCS
  features share the vessel-scale dimension but remain complementary
  through the temporal dynamics channel.
\item \textbf{Cargo-to-Tanker confusion drops from 0.2\% to
  0.1\%} after fusion; Tanker-to-Cargo from 0.1\% to 0.1\%.
\item \textbf{Classical ensemble methods scale more than HQNN}
  from kinematic augmentation, attributed to unconstrained feature
  interaction capacity. The quantum advantage in this problem remains
  in calibration (uncertainty quantification).
\end{enumerate}

The nine-feature kinematic-RCS descriptor is derived entirely from
standard coastal radar tracking outputs, requires no additional
hardware, and activates after a 15-minute track warm-up period that
is operationally acceptable in maritime surveillance.
